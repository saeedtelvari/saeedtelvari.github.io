Story homepage: three art directions

Each direction shows two frames from the storyboard: the opening view from orbit, and the frame where the 3D grid becomes VE columns.

Move the slider, or select Play, to remove the vertical cells in all three VE frames. The rock keeps its 3D thickness; each column becomes one cell with a sharp CO₂ interface. The coastlines come from Natural Earth (public domain). The plume is illustrative.

There is more than 420 ppm of CO₂ in the air. I model how rock under the North Sea can hold it for thousands of years.

280 ppm in 1850. More than 420 ppm today.

A 3D model divides the reservoir into cells. Field models have millions of cells, and one run can take days.

CO₂ is lighter than brine, so it rises to the top of each column. A VE model keeps one number per column, and runs up to 100× faster.

  • DescentThe camera zooms through chart scales: 1:10 000 000, then 1:500 000, then a harbour-plan view of the platform.
  • InjectionThe well is a chart sounding line. The layers below it are BGS lithology hatching.
  • 3D problemA line-art grid. The run clock is a printed scale bar that fills.
  • Live VEThe plume is a set of magenta contour bands, like depth contours, that grow each year.
