One key frame for each of the six chapters, in art direction C (textbook cutaway). The scroll moves the visitor from one frame to the next.

Frames 2 to 5 show one depleted gas field: a sandstone dome with a methane gas cap at the crest, a fault on one flank, and a CO₂ plume from a well on the other flank. The shapes and the numbers are illustrative.

2. Into the rock

The sandstone is a dome that held natural gas for millions of years. Most of the gas is produced, so the dome can now store CO₂.

  • Into this frameThe camera follows the well down. The first caption shows on the way: "The well goes about 2 km down, through clay, chalk and a shale caprock."

3. Rock to grid

Scroll 44 to 60%

18 h 40 min

3D run time so far, for a typical field model

A 3D model splits the reservoir into cells that follow the layers and the fault. Field models have millions of cells, and one run can take days.

  • Into this frameThe overburden fades. Grid lines draw onto the sandstone: pillars first, then layers.
  • In the frameThe plume grows near the well, and the gas cap moves a little. The clock counts up.
  • Second caption"Faults cut the reservoir and can leak. In the MuPSI project, which studies fault integrity, I apply these models to depleted gas fields."

4. 3D to VE

Scroll 60 to 80%

to solve, not 720 cells

The rock stays 3D, but the vertical cells go. VE solves one value per column, the plume thickness h, so it runs up to 100× faster.

  • Into this frameThe same block as frame 3. The horizontal cell lines fade out. The block keeps its height.
  • In the frameEach column is one cell. Gas fills it down to h: red for methane, blue for CO₂. The top shows h as stepped bands.
  • First caption"Gas is lighter than brine. Both the CO₂ and the methane rise to the top of each column."
